Yellow Leaves Diagnosis

Yellow leaves are a message. Let's translate it.

Getting started

Nothing panics a new plant parent like yellowing leaves, and the internet usually makes it worse — too many guesses, all stated with total confidence. This worksheet turns the panic into a calm process: describe what you actually see, test the soil, rule out the usual suspects one by one, and write down what you did so next time starts closer to the answer.

Print it right

  • US Letter paper, portrait, 100 % scale (“Actual size”, never “Fit to page”).
  • Print a few and keep them with the plant profiles — one worksheet per mystery.
  • If you can, snap a photo before you remove any leaves; patterns in the whole plant matter.

How to work the case

  • Tick every pattern that matches — several can be true at once.
  • Do the finger test on the soil before you water again; it is the single best clue.
  • The worksheet walks you to a top suspect, an action, and a follow-up date.
Remember: one yellow leaf now and then — even a cheerful curve at the bottom of an otherwise happy plant — is just a leaf retiring. Diagnose patterns, not individual leaves.

3 · The suspect line-up

Bottom leaves one or two at a time, plant otherwise healthy Natural aging — or a long dry spell. Verify with the soil test.
Soil constantly wet, drooping, stems going squishy Overwatering — let it dry properly, check the drainage hole.
Crisp edges, dry soil, quicker leaf drop Underwatering or very low humidity — water thoroughly on schedule.
Pale and washed-out all over, little new growth Not enough light — move nearer a bright window.
Veins stay green, leaf yellows between them Hungry — feed at half strength during the growing season.
Sudden mass yellowing or dots, webs, sticky spots Shock, cold, or pests — inspect under the leaves with a light.
