Fertilizer Date Log

Feed gently, on schedule, and write it down.

Getting started

Most houseplants are not dying from a lack of food — they are suffering from twice feeding on a sunny Tuesday and then a three-month gap, or from one enthusiastic pour of full-strength plant food. This log keeps every feeding on the page, so a light hand at steady intervals replaces the feast-and-famine cycle.

Print it right

  • US Letter paper, portrait, 100 % scale (“Actual size”, never “Fit to page”).
  • Keep the sheet with the watering rotation — feeding rides along on watering day.
  • One sheet covers about two months for a small collection; print a couple at a time.

How to fill it in

  • Record what the label actually says, then dilute to half strength unless the plant is a known heavy feeder.
  • Feed only during spring and summer growth; taper off in fall and stop in winter.
  • “Response” is what the plant did over the next week or two — new leaf, no change, brown tips. It teaches you the next cycle.
Tip: Never feed a bone-dry plant, a freshly repotted one, or a sick one. Water first (or wait), and let the roots settle. Feeding is fertilizer for healthy plants, not medicine for struggling ones.

Season:   Remember: half the strength on the bottle, less often than you think.

Reading the label: N–P–K in plain words

N (nitrogen) = leaves. P (phosphorus) = roots and blooms. K (potassium) = overall health. A balanced houseplant food (numbers near each other, e.g. 10-10-10) at half strength covers almost everything.

Feed windows

Spring–summer: every 2–4 weeks, and always after watering. Fall: taper to once a month. Winter: skip — resting plants cannot drink it.

Feeding record

Date Plant Food + dilution Amount Response over next weeks Next feed

Brown, crispy leaf tips soon after feeding usually mean the dose was too strong — pause, flush the pot with plain water, and halve the strength next time.
