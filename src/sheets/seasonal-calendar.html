Seasonal Care Calendar Strip

Your year in plant care, one season at a time.

Getting started

Houseplants do not keep the same calendar you do. The indoor seasons — the furnace weeks, the bright-late-spring afternoons, the dormant stretch when nothing grows no matter how dutiful you are — are what actually set the rhythm of watering, feeding, repotting, and moving plants around. This landscape strip lays out the whole year in four panels, so you always know what it is time for.

Print it right

  • US Letter paper, landscape orientation, 100 % scale (“Actual size”).
  • Print one copy on the fridge or inside a cabinet door — the four panels read best mounted wide.
  • Reprint in January and pencil in your own dates as the year unfolds.

How to use it

  • Read only the panel you are in today; ignore the rest until they are your season.
  • Use the “my dates” lines to pencil real beginnings — first feed, last feed, and so on.
  • Pair with the Fertilizer Date Log and the Repotting Checklist at the season’s changes.
Tip: When light changes — spring and fall — plants drink differently. Shift watering by a few days before shifting anything else, and most seasonal problems resolve themselves.

Seasonal Care Calendar Strip

Northern-hemisphere defaults; nudge a month either way for your own windows and radiators. Indoor season, pot-speak edition.

Spring

March · April · May

  • Growth restarts — water slowly wakes up with the light.
  • Resume feeding, half strength, monthly first.
  • Best repot window: roots soon, potting mix fresh.
  • Gradual sun-tolerance check: move sun-shy plants back from sudden glass.
  • Take cuttings for the propagation jars.
  • My dates: first feed
  • First repot

Summer

June · July · August

  • Peak growing: expect to water more often than you think.
  • Feed every 2–4 weeks on watering day.
  • Sun deflectors on: sheer curtains stop leaf scorch from afternoon glass.
  • Vacations made easy: fill in a Plant-Sitter Card before you go.
  • Watch heat stress: crispy edges, faded colors.
  • My dates: summer feed
  • Away dates

Fall

September · October · November

  • Growth tapers — so does drinking: stretch intervals.
  • Feed once more, then taper to a stop by the end of fall.
  • Bringing plants indoors: check under leaves for stowaway pests first.
  • Rotate plants a quarter-turn weekly as light direction shifts.
  • Last call for repotting and rearrangements — settle everyone in place for winter.
  • My dates: last feed
  • Plants moved in

Winter

December · January · February

  • Furnace season: air dries, soil stays wet longer — water far less.
  • No feeding: resting plants cannot use it.
  • Move plants closer to windows or add a lamp; short days are the real winter.
  • Keep off cold drafts and away from radiators; dry, hot air bakes leaves faster than the roots can replace it.
  • Browse, plan, and order: the sheets you print now become spring in motion.
  • My dates: rest window
  • Light check

The general law: more light, more growth, more water, more food — and every fall and spring, half of your plant care is simply relearning where the light went.
