Repotting Checklist

A calm, step-by-step move-in day for your plant.

Getting started

Repotting frightens people more than it frightens plants, and it usually goes wrong for small avoidable reasons: a pot that is too big, a day that was already stressful, scissors that were never sharpened. This checklist turns the job into three short stages — before, during, after — so you can put a tick next to each step and relax your way through it.

Print it right

  • US Letter paper, portrait, 100 % scale (“Actual size”, never “Fit to page”).
  • Print one sheet per repotting session and tape it somewhere near the mess.
  • You will be working with wet hands — the sheet is fine, but keep it out of the soil pile.

Before you begin

  • Skip repotting a plant that is already sick or showing new yellowing; stabilize first.
  • Spring is move-in season — plants are waking up and recover fastest then.
Tip: New pot only a little larger than the old one — two to five centimeters wider. A pot with room to spare is a swimming pool in miniature: the extra soil holds water the roots cannot yet drink and quietly rots them.

Repotting Checklist

Plant:   Date:   Old pot size → new:

A few days before

  • Water the plant so the rootball is easy to slide out.
  • New pot is 2–5 cm wider and has a drainage hole.
  • Fresh potting mix on hand, plus a little plain bark or perlite for drainage.
  • Newspaper, a tarp, or a big tray laid down for the mess.
  • Scissors, trowel or spoon, gloves, and a label with the plant’s name.

During (do this gently, and stop if a root fights back)

  • Tip the pot sideways, squeeze or rap the sides, slide the rootball out — never yank the stem.
  • Loosen the outer roots gently with fingers; snip only dead, spiraling, or mushy roots.
  • Scrub or rinse the old pot if reusing it — no salt crust on the sides.
  • Crumble away the loosest third of old mix only — do not strip the rootball bare.
  • Pot sits at the same depth as before; the crown of the plant is not buried.
  • Add mix down the sides, tap to settle, then water once, well.

The days after (quiet observation week)

  • Keep out of direct sun and away from cold drafts for about a week.
  • Hold the fertilizer — wait until new leaves appear (this is often 2–4 weeks).
  • Some drooping drama on day one is normal; deep shriveling that worsens is a call-back.
  • Log the date on a Plant Profile Card or the Fertilizer Date Log.

Sure signs it is repot time

  • Roots circling out of the drainage hole or surfacing on top.
  • Water runs through in seconds — the rootball crowds all the soil.
  • The plant dries out within a day of watering.
  • Growth has stalled a full season for no other reason.
