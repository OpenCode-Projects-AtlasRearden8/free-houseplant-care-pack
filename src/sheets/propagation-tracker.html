Getting started

Snipping a friend’s pothos and growing your own is one of the quiet joys of houseplants, and water propagation is where beginners should start: you can literally watch the roots arrive. This tracker keeps the methodical part — which jar is which, when the water last changed, when roots showed up — on one page, so ten jars stay ten experiments instead of ten mysteries.

Print it right

  • US Letter paper, portrait, 100 % scale (“Actual size”, never “Fit to page”).
  • Number your jars with tape + a marker, then use the same numbers here.
  • One sheet comfortably covers a shelf of jars; reprint for the next batch.

Basics for a happy jar

  • Cut just below a node (the bump leaves grow from) and keep that node underwater.
  • Bright indirect light, room temperature, and tap water — let chlorine breathe off overnight if you can.
  • Snip anything soft; one rotting stem can sour the whole jar.
Tip: When roots reach about 3 cm, pot them up into damp mix and keep humidity high for a week (a clear bag loosely tented over works). Water roots and soil roots speak different dialects — expect a short pouting period.

Reading the water

Cloudy water is overdue for a change; a whisper of algae is survivable, but a slimy stem is a signal — trim back to healthy tissue, give the jar a proper wash, and restart with fresh water. Change the water about weekly, without letting the leafy part of the cutting ever sit submerged.

Best first tries

Pothos, philodendron, tradescantia, or a stubborn-hearted monstera node. Tall fussy plants — skipping ahead to soil is also allowed.
